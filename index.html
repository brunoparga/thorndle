ÞORNDLE

The four English th's

    Þ þ thorn the unvoiced th of thin and bath — /θ/
    Ð ð edh the voiced th of this and father — /ð/

    Type [ for þ, ] for ð

    How to play

    Guess the five-letter word in seven tries. After each guess the tiles change colour: green for the right letter in the right place, gold for the right letter in the wrong place, black for a letter that is not in the word at all. Keys you have not tried yet stay grey.

    Þ and ð are letters here, not pairs. Wherever English writes a th that is really one sound, Þorndle writes one tile. So worth is WORÞ and too short to play, while months is MONÞS and fits perfectly — six-letter words are where most of the good ones come from.

    Which of the two you need is a matter of sound, not spelling. Say the word: if your vocal cords buzz, it is ð (mother, rather, smooth); if it is a plain hiss, it is þ (thanks, health, throat).

    And a th that is neither is not a digraph at all, so it keeps both its letters. Thyme is said /taɪm/ — a plain t with a silent h — and so it is spelled with five tiles, t·h·y·m·e, and plays. Same for the t and h that merely meet across a seam, as in pothole. Putting t next to h is a claim about pronunciation, and now and then it is the right one.

    On a physical keyboard, type [ for þ and ] for ð.
